Overview

DexJiT examines how prediction targets, sampling, and training duration affect dexterous motion generation. Using a JiT-based Transformer [1], we compare clean-motion (x), velocity (v), and noise (ε) prediction on HandX [2], generating 56 future frames from four observations under a shared velocity loss. The x/v ranking changes with both training duration and endpoint treatment: v has lower validation FID at 5k updates, while x leads at 15k and 30k; changing only the endpoint sampling rule also reverses their ranking. Velocity damping reduces trajectory spread by 28–34% but worsens FID and energy score. Under the final 30k test protocol, x achieves FID 1.980, compared with 2.204 for v and 2.386 for ε. These experiments show that the observed advantage of a prediction target depends on how long it is trained and how its outputs are sampled.

Metric definitions+

FID

Distance between real and generated motion distributions in HandX feature space.

ES

A forecast score combining distance to the recorded future and distances between samples, normalized by trajectory dimension.

Sample / mean / median ADE

Joint-position error for individual samples, their mean, or the geometric median at each joint and frame.

Best-of-20 ADE

The lowest trajectory error among 20 samples, selected using the recorded future.

Endpoint sampling

With weights and initial noise fixed, denominator clipping gives v the lower FID; velocity damping gives x the lower FID. Damping reduces trajectory spread and sample ADE, while increasing FID and energy score.

Sampling rules and pairwise distance+

Sampling time t runs from noise at 0 to motion at 1. Denominator clipping limits small denominators to 0.05 when converting x or ε to velocity; direct v predictions are unchanged. Velocity damping multiplies all converted velocities by min(1, t/0.05, (1−t)/0.05). Pairwise distance is the mean distance between flattened sample trajectories, divided by the square root of their dimension.
